# Layout principles for branded image composition

## 1. Establish the visual sentence

Every composition should answer, in order:

1. What is the main thing?
2. What frames or explains it?
3. Where does the eye enter and leave?
4. Which area must remain quiet?

If two elements answer question 1, reduce one before adding detail.

## 2. Framing and quiet field

- Place the approved focal element first and reserve its clearspace.
- Use edge weight, foreground layers, or a proscenium only when they support the concept.
- Keep one deliberately quiet field around copy, product, person, or mark.
- Let supporting silhouettes overlap enough to read as one spatial system, not pasted strips.
- Use color locally and hierarchically; avoid unrelated background halves.
- Verify the framing at thumbnail size and at intended viewing distance.

Project-specific devices such as a skyline, route, district palette, or branded pill belong in that project's rules, not in this reusable reference.

## 3. Marks, paths, and decorative lines

- Place official marks and exact copy before decorative paths or lines.
- Route decoration around clearspace; never slice through letters, QR codes, faces, product edges, or safety information.
- Keep dashed lines and small motifs away from counters and thin strokes.
- At thumbnail size, the decoration must remain secondary.

## 4. Generated material versus official material

Generated layers may provide architecture, atmospheric texture, illustration, or decorative silhouettes. They must not provide final logos or readable copy.

Before composition, remove generated pseudo-text, fake signage, artifacts, and unintended shadows. Match generated elements to official material through crop, scale, color grade, mask, and overlap rather than asking the generator to recreate the complete final layout.

## 5. Common failure patterns

### Split-panel accident

Symptoms: gradient upper half, white lower strip, and a hard horizon unrelated to the brief.

Fix: extend the shared background behind all layers; cut out the skyline; overlap it into the brand field; add a shared baseline or haze only if needed.

### Blank moat

Symptoms: a large empty band separates the logo from the framing city.

Fix: raise selected skyline peaks, lower the mark slightly, or introduce a quiet middle-distance layer. Do not fill the moat with random icons.

### Decoration through content

Symptoms: dotted route crosses the headline or wordmark and makes letters look damaged.

Fix: reroute behind the clearspace or interrupt the route invisibly beneath the content mask.

### Equal visual weight

Symptoms: logo, skyline, doodles, and copy all demand attention at once.

Fix: choose one focal layer; lower saturation, contrast, or scale of the rest.

### Pale foreground loss

Symptoms: background remover deletes white buildings, glass, smoke, or highlights.

Fix: compare semantic removal with known-color keying, use a larger model, restore the alpha mask locally, and inspect on black/white checkerboards.

## 6. Proofing routine

Review four proofs:

1. Thumbnail without guides: hierarchy only.
2. Grayscale thumbnail: contrast and focal order.
3. Safe-area proof: trim, seams, and clearspace.
4. 100% edge crop on checkerboard: mask quality and resolution.
